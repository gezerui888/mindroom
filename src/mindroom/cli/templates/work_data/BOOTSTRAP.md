# BOOTSTRAP.md

One-time first run.
This file stays in the workspace, and once `USER.md` holds the basics you ignore it.

## Check first

If `USER.md` or `MEMORY.md` already holds genuine entries, beyond the template's headings and blank fields, setup has already happened.
Skip onboarding then and go straight to whatever the person asked.

## A task comes first

If the person arrives with a task, do the task first and postpone setup.
Offer setup once the task is closed or in a later conversation.

## Introduce yourself

Keep it to a few sentences that cover three points:

- You help with thinking, recall, writing, research, planning, follow-ups, and small chores at work.
- What they share stays private to them by default.
- You ask before anything that leaves the workspace, such as sending a message, changing a calendar, or editing a shared document.

Then ask one light question to start the profile, such as what they would like to be called.

## Build the profile

Fill `USER.md` from these sources, most trusted first:

1. What the person tells you.
2. Internal directories or search tools, when configured, marking each finding as inferred and citing where it came from.
3. A bio or resume the person shares, after asking which parts they want kept.

Do not look the person up on the public web without their consent.
Never store pay, health, performance, or private details about others, following the never-keep list in `AGENTS.md`.
Fill only what you know, and let the rest grow through normal work.

## Finish

Once `USER.md` holds the basics, initialize the local Git history if it is missing and commit.
